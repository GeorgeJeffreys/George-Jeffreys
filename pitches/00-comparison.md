## Which two are strongest

**03 (small-cap cash merger arbitrage) and 01 (clustered insider purchases)** are the strongest, and for the same underlying reason: each names a counterparty who is structurally prevented from taking the money, rather than one who has merely not noticed it. In 03 the absent buyer is the merger-arb fund whose ticket size cannot fit a $300m target, which is a capacity argument that does not decay with publication. In 01 the absent buyer is the quant mandate that cannot deploy $5k positions in $200m companies, and Cziraki and Gider's finding that insider returns fall with trade and firm size is precisely the shape an uncompeted retail-scale edge should have. Both are long-only, so the borrow constraint that hangs over the DCE's short leg never arises; both have commission drag under 50 bp; both have a kill test that costs under a week on WRDS and returns a number that either ends the project or does not. Neither claims to beat the benchmark on Sharpe, and 03's own pitch places itself at exactly the DCE's 0.5–1.0. Their case rests on buildability, capital efficiency and falsifiability, which is the honest place for it to rest. The serious caveat on 03 is compliance: it is the worst possible strategy for an incoming banker to have any beneficial interest in, and its pitch says so.

## Which are weakest

**05 (XBRL fundamentals) and 02 (index deletions)** are the weakest, and each pitch more or less concedes it. 05 is an honest post-mortem: Chen and Velikov's 93% post-cost decay, Hou, Xue and Zhang's microcap dependence, and Engelberg, McLean and Pontiff's finding that anomaly returns concentrate on the earnings-announcement day, which precedes the XBRL filing, leave a residual that the agent values at an information ratio of 0.3–0.5 and that would take twenty years to distinguish from zero. 02 is a well-researched pitch whose own strongest objection is correct: once Greenwood and Sammon have shown the event-day effect is gone, what remains is a twelve-month small-cap value and long-term reversal exposure with an exogenous timestamp, and the pitch's own decay arithmetic (Research Affiliates' 5% p.a. haircut by 58% to roughly 2%) does not support the 0.3–0.7 Sharpe it then claims. 04 sits in the middle: a real, free, timestamped feed with a genuine parsing edge, but its only refereed evidence says the effect is large-cap driven, and the red-team file shows its kill threshold would pass a strategy that loses money after costs. 06 is the most interesting of the remaining three but carries the worst cost structure and the worst tail, and the 2026 earnings season is a live warning that the regime can flip.

## Which could run alongside the Disclosure Change Engine

Three of the six share the DCE's pipeline almost exactly (EDGAR polling, LLM extraction into Supabase, `ib_async` market-on-close execution against an ETF or futures hedge), so the marginal build is a signal module rather than a system. **03 is the best complement**: its return stream is deal-completion risk, which is uncorrelated with text-change under-reaction except in a crash, and its EDGAR full-text-search poller and Exhibit 2.1 term extractor are the DCE's tooling pointed at a different form type. **01 is the best signal layer** rather than a separate book: an insider-purchase cluster arriving after the DCE flags a negative disclosure change is a disagreement signal that the DCE can use to size or veto its short, and a cluster with no negative change is a long candidate on shared infrastructure. **05 is a filter, not a strategy**, as its own pitch says: the fundamentals it computes stop the DCE shorting cash-rich names on a language change alone, and that is worth the three-day test even though the standalone book is not. 02 and 04 are also long-only small-cap with an ETF hedge, so adding them to 01 and the DCE's long leg concentrates the whole portfolio in one factor (small-cap reversal and value) that drew down together in 2020–21; they diversify infrastructure but not risk. 06 is the mirror image: it shares no data, no code and no factor with anything else, which makes it the only genuinely uncorrelated return stream on the list, and the only one that cannot borrow a line of the DCE's build. If the goal is uncorrelated signals on shared infrastructure, the order is 03, then 01 as a layer, then 05 as a filter, with 06 as the one standalone worth a three-day kill test on OptionMetrics before anything is built.
