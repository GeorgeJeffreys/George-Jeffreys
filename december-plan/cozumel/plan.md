# Cozumel diving plan — Wharton Outdoors Club, 8–15 Dec 2026

Workstream W1. Researched 21 Sep 2026 by web search only (operator sites were unreachable through the proxy, so every figure below is from a search-result snippet and is flagged ADVERTISED (snippet), QUOTE NEEDED or INFERRED). Prices USD with MXN alongside where the operator lists both; operators use roughly 17.5–18.3 MXN/USD.

## Conclusions

1. Approach Dive Paradise first for the 30- and 40-person scenarios (11 boats including two 44-ft boats carrying 16 divers each, 1 guide per 8, Wyndham lodging bundle) and Blue Note Scuba first for the 20-person scenario (cheapest documented certified package: 5 days/10 dives USD 600 / MXN 10,500; OW USD 400 / MXN 7,000 plus eLearning). Sand Dollar Sports is the strongest beginner-training site (own beach, shallow shore dives that still run when the port is closed) and should be quoted for the Open Water block in every scenario.
2. Diving-only cost per head (INFERRED from advertised figures, excluding flights, lodging and food): certified ≈ USD 830 (package 600 + park fee 75 + gear 125 + refresher 30); beginner ≈ USD 1,000 (course 400 + eLearning ≈ 195–230 + park fee 75 + two fun days 260 + gear 50); advanced ≈ USD 1,350–1,500 (package + AOW ≈ 530 + Deep/Drift/Night specialties QUOTE NEEDED). That leaves ≈ USD 1,170 (certified) and ≈ USD 1,300 (beginner) for flights plus seven nights, so the USD 2,000/2,300 ceilings hold only with cheap flights and shared rooms; the advanced pathway is likely to breach USD 2,000 and should be priced as an opt-in supplement.
3. Capacity, not price, is the binding constraint: at 40 people no single small-boat operator on the island can seat 20 certified divers, 20 students and 3 instructors in one morning departure. Plan on two operators (one training-led, one boat-led) or on Dive Paradise's big boats, and get written confirmation of instructor numbers for 10/15/20 students (PADI 8:1 on open-water training dives, 10:1 in confined water).
4. Weather: December sits in norther season (nine port closures logged in Decembers 2010–2017 on ScubaBoard; closures usually last one to two days). The Mon 14 spare day is essential for beginners' certification dives; the 5-night version has no buffer and should be sold as "certification not guaranteed if the port closes".
5. Every operator needs a written quote: none publishes a group rate, a free organiser place, per-participant payment links or an insurance certificate. Outreach emails (one per operator) are in `outreach/`.

Fit against advertised fleets (INFERRED):

Practical structure: for 30–40, split the contract — a training operator (Sand Dollar or Blue Note) for the 10–20 students Wed–Fri and a boat operator (Dive Paradise or Aldora) for the certified group Wed–Fri, then everyone on the boat operator's big boats Sat–Sun. ## Norther contingency

- Trigger and duration: the Port Captain closes the port at sustained winds above ≈18 kn or ≈4 ft seas; closures are usually by vessel class (small craft first), sometimes geographic (east coast/north tip only); boats over 48 ft are sometimes allowed out; closures peak Dec–Feb, usually hours to a day, occasionally two. ScubaBoard's log counts nine December closures in 2010–2017. A front is visible in forecasts about three days out.
- Operator terms found: Blue Note full refund or new date for weather; Deep Blue full refund for weather; Scuba Life full refund only if the Harbour Master closes the port; Dive Boutique and ScubaLuis reschedule/credit/refund. Dive Paradise, Aldora, Sand Dollar, Salty, Blue Magic, Scuba Club and ScubaTony: QUOTE NEEDED (all asked in outreach).
- Fallbacks: Sand Dollar shore dives to 30 ft at Coral Gardens, SNUBA and restoration tours; Dive Paradise welcome-tank shore dive at the Wyndham pier; confined-water sessions for students; land day (San Gervasio, Punta Sur, Chankanaab). Big-boat operators (Dive Paradise 44-ft) have the best chance of sailing on a marginal day.
- Schedule buffers: Mon 14 spare in the 7-night plan; none in the 5-night plan. Beginners' OW dives 3–4 must have Sat and Mon as fallback days. Ask each operator to commit in writing to prioritising students' certification dives on the first open day.

## Risks

1. Capacity: no single small-boat operator can carry 40; contract two operators or Dive Paradise's big boats. Ratio compliance (8:1 on OW dives) needs 2–3 instructors dedicated to the club for three days — confirm names and PADI numbers.
2. Price uncertainty: seven of nine price fields per operator are QUOTE NEEDED; Blue Note's two OW figures conflict; Blue Magic's published seasons skip 16–20 Dec and 8–15 Dec sits in the low-season band but may be repriced.
3. Blue Magic ownership: "is for Sale" page live; deposits at risk if the business changes hands.
4. Budget: advanced pathway likely exceeds USD 2,000 all-in; beginners' eLearning (≈USD 195–230 from PADI) is on top of every course price and must be bought before travel.
5. Organiser place: no operator advertises a free leader place; if none is granted, George's diving (≈USD 830) spread over 19/29/39 payers adds ≈USD 44/29/21 per head and must be disclosed.
6. Weather: two-day closure Fri–Sat in the 5-night plan leaves beginners uncertified; offer PADI referral completion at home.
7. Bull-shark day: baited; prerequisites exclude new divers; ferry timing and December sea state can cancel it; keep it fully optional and paid separately.
8. Marine park fee is USD 15 / MXN 220 per day, cash, excluded by almost every operator (some sites still show USD 11–14) — budget it separately.
9. Insurance: no operator's liability certificate or PADI status was verifiable by search; DAN dive-accident cover per participant should be a club requirement (price not researched).
