## Conclusions

1. No public MBA club-travel rule exists. The closest documented analogue (Wharton undergraduate clubs) bans travel on class, reading and exam days, requires a signed Acknowledgement of Risks & Hold Harmless form, group flights, a trip notification form and a Penn Global petition for heightened-risk regions, and limits social retreats to 50 miles of campus. If MBA Student Life applies anything like it, the 19–24 Nov Thanksgiving alternative is dead and Cozumel 8–15 Dec (four Penn exam weekdays) is exposed; the 25–29 Nov and 8–13 Dec versions are the defensible ones. Send the email in `email_student_life.md` now; approval lead time is unknown.
2. Penn Global rules bite only if the trip is "Penn-affiliated" (faculty present, Penn or Penn-managed funds, credit or research). A participant-funded club trip is probably not, which means no MyTrips duty, no CITRA petition (graduate students are exempt anyway) and, importantly, no Penn International SOS cover: everyone must buy their own. Egypt at Level 2 is not a heightened-risk destination; the Sinai Level 4 zones are avoided by basing in Marsa Alam or Hurghada.
3. Entry: all eight passports can enter all four destinations without a consular visa provided the Indian and Chinese holders carry a valid US visa (Mexico and Bahamas waive on that basis; Egypt issues e-visas to both; Puerto Rico is domestic). Egypt costs USD 30 (visa on arrival or e-visa, since 1 Mar 2026); Mexico's FMM fee is in the air ticket; Bahamas needs a passport from everyone, including US citizens.
4. Insurance: mandate DAN membership (USD 40) plus the Preferred plan (USD 79, USD 250,000 per accident, secondary) for every diver, with Guardian (USD 119) for the Tiger Beach group; DiveAssure is the alternative for anyone who wants a one-week plan. PSIP/Aetna is not a substitute: chambers abroad want a guarantee of payment that DAN gives and Aetna does not. Trip cancellation for Egypt is only worth buying with a CFAR rider (50–75 percent back, buy within 14–21 days of the first deposit) because the Middle East war has been a known event since 28 Feb 2026 and Generali (DAN's carrier) will not write Middle East trips at all.
5. Money: collect through CampusGroups (Stripe, 2.9 percent + USD 0.30) or the operator's own per-person links; never through a personal Venmo/Zelle, whose weekly caps (USD 4,999.99 person-to-person on Venmo) and personal-liability optics are wrong for a 20–40 person trip. Disclose any organiser free place in the sign-up page and let the club, not George, decide whether it is spread as a discount.

## 2. Penn Global rules for graduate travellers

- Definition of Penn-affiliated travel: travel in the course of Penn employment; accompanied by Penn faculty or staff; directly related to academic research or fieldwork; "student travel funded by Penn or Penn-managed funds"; or education abroad for Penn credit. DOCUMENTED (snippet). Source: https://global.upenn.edu/gss/travel-guidance/register-your-trip/penn-affiliated-travel/. A participant-funded club trip with no faculty is affiliated only if WGA/club money counts as "Penn-managed funds". MyTrips is for international travel only; the insurance "is only available outside the US (but does include coverage in Puerto Rico)". DOCUMENTED (snippet). - Personal travel: cover "applies to current faculty, staff, and students while on Penn-affiliated travel abroad"; others "are not eligible for Penn insurance and are expected to acquire their own travel medical, evacuation, and repatriation insurance". DOCUMENTED (snippet). Source: https://global.upenn.edu/gss/travel-guidance/insurance-and-24-7-assistance-abroad/travel-insurance/. ## 5. Trip cancellation insurance and the Egypt known-event problem

Facts.
- The Middle East war became a known event on 28 Feb 2026; Allianz lists Egypt among the Impacted Countries and its accommodations apply only to plans effective on or before 28 Feb 2026; plans bought from 1 Mar 2026 exclude war-related losses there. DOCUMENTED (snippet), https://www.allianztravelinsurance.com/coverage-alerts/war-in-middle-east-surrounding-region.htm.
- Standard plans exclude war, military action, government airspace closures and cancellations caused by travel warnings; Squaremouth's advice is CFAR and IFAR. DOCUMENTED (snippet), https://www.squaremouth.com/travel-advice/travel-insurance-coverage-for-military-action.
- Generali (DAN's trip-insurance carrier) states its plans "are not available for trips to Israel, Ukraine, all destinations in the Middle East" and excludes known or foreseeable events. DOCUMENTED (snippet), https://www.generalitravelinsurance.com/travel-resources/not-covered.html; DAN Travel Protection: https://apps.dan.org/trip-insurance/. Whether Generali classes Egypt as Middle East is QUOTE NEEDED; assume yes.
- Seven Corners Trip Protection: Basic excludes acts of war; Choice reimburses up to 100 percent for covered reasons and offers optional CFAR at 75 percent within the purchase window. DOCUMENTED (snippet), https://www.sevencorners.com/plans/trip-protection/basic-choice. Egypt availability QUOTE NEEDED.
- Travel Guard (now Zurich-owned) sells Egypt plans and offers CFAR up to 75 percent on its Deluxe plan. DOCUMENTED (snippet), https://www.travelguard.com/travel-resources/destinations/africa-and-middle-east/egypt. A Travel Guard Middle East alert was not found; QUOTE NEEDED.
- CFAR mechanics (Squaremouth): optional rider paying 50–75 percent of insured non-refundable cost; buy within 14–21 days of the first trip payment; insure the full trip cost; cancel at least 48 hours before departure; CFAR plans cost roughly 40–50 percent more than a comprehensive plan. DOCUMENTED (snippet), https://www.squaremouth.com/travel-insurance-benefits/cancel-for-any-reason and https://www.squaremouth.com/resources/best-travel-insurance/cancel-for-any-reason. CFAR is not sold in every US state (New York in particular); QUOTE NEEDED per participant.

What it means. For Cozumel, Puerto Rico and the Bahamas, an ordinary comprehensive plan bought on the Squaremouth marketplace within the CFAR window is adequate and the war exclusion is irrelevant. For Egypt, a conflict flare-up, airspace closure over the eastern Mediterranean or a State Department move to Level 3 would not be a covered reason on any policy bought after 28 Feb 2026; only CFAR pays, and only 50–75 percent. So the Egypt deposit structure has to carry the risk that insurance will not.

Contract-term protections for the Egypt (and every) trip, all INFERRED practice unless flagged.
1. Deposit size and timing: pay the smallest deposit the operator will accept (Red Sea Diving Safari's terms QUOTE NEEDED) and hold the club's own deposit call until flights are confirmed bookable at the budgeted fare; take participant money in two or three instalments (deposit; balance at 60 days; extras on site).
2. Refundable or transferable: ask for a deposit that is refundable up to a fixed date and thereafter transferable to another date within 12 months or to a named substitute; a "credit note" is worth less than cash but better than forfeiture.
3. Force majeure clause naming the triggers: war or armed conflict in Egypt or on the flight path, closure of Egyptian or transit airspace, a State Department Level 3 or higher advisory for the Red Sea governorate, or a Penn/Wharton prohibition on the trip; remedy = full refund or free transfer.
4. Name changes free until a late date (ask for 14 days before arrival) so a drop-out can be replaced from the waitlist; this is the cheapest insurance the club can buy.
5. Pay by credit card, in the participant's own name where possible, for chargeback rights under the Fair Credit Billing Act if the service is not delivered; expect a surcharge (QUOTE NEEDED; 3–5 percent is common with Mexican and Egyptian operators, INFERRED) and weigh it against the protection.
6. Group airfare: carriers treat 10+ passengers on a segment as a group; deposits typically 25–40 percent and usually non-refundable; names due about 21–30 days before departure with limited free changes (some contracts allow around 10 percent of the group to change up to a month out); Frontier's public terms are USD 40 per person deposit within 5 business days and USD 50 name changes inside 21 days. ADVERTISED (snippet), https://grouptravelnetwork.com/group-airfare-guide/, https://www.flyfrontier.com/groups. Legacy carriers' group desks set terms case by case. For Egypt via Europe, a group contract with a European carrier is attractive only if it includes a war/airspace refund clause; otherwise let participants book individually on refundable-fare classes or with points.
7. Documentation: keep every operator email confirming the terms above; if the operator cannot put force majeure in writing, cap the pre-March exposure at the deposit and choose the refundable room rate even if dearer.

## 8. Risk register (top five per trip)

A. Thanksgiving, La Parguera (Tiger Beach variant in brackets)
1. Class-day conflict if 19–24 Nov is chosen: probable refusal under any rule resembling the undergraduate one. Mitigation: 25–29 Nov. ASK STUDENT LIFE.
2. Chamber distance: nearest DCS-capable chamber may be San Juan (2 h+). Mitigation: conservative profiles, oxygen on the boat, operator's evacuation plan in writing, DAN for all.
3. Weather: late-November fronts close the south-west shelf. Mitigation: refundable or transferable dive days.
4. International students routed through a foreign hub: converts domestic to international travel. Mitigation: nonstop or US-only connections.
5. [Tiger Beach] Baited shark dive: liability releases waive operator negligence; insurance position unconfirmed; nearest chamber Nassau or Florida; 26 nm offshore. Mitigation: Guardian plan, written DAN confirmation, 18 h no-fly, cap at experienced divers, and disclose "baited shark dive" in every document.

B. December, Cozumel
1. Exam-period overlap (Thu 10, Fri 11, Mon 14, Tue 15 Dec) and possible rule against exam-day travel. Mitigation: 8–13 Dec version; ASK STUDENT LIFE.
2. Beginner ratio (about half doing Open Water, up to 20 students): needs 2–3 instructors and confined water; medical-form "yes" answers surface late. Mitigation: medical forms and eLearning before the deposit; instructor count in the contract.
3. Visa waiver dependence: Indian/Chinese participants whose US visa stamp has expired cannot use the waiver. Mitigation: passport and visa audit at sign-up; SRE appointment lead time.
4. Northers closing the port. Mitigation: operator with larger boats, flexible dive-day credits.
5. Money handling at scale (up to 40 people, USD 100k+): chargebacks, double charges, personal-account collection. Mitigation: CampusGroups deposit, operator links for balances, written refund policy.

C. January, Egypt
1. Known-event exclusion: no post-28 Feb 2026 policy pays for war, airspace or advisory cancellations; only CFAR at 50–75 percent. Mitigation: contract protections in section 5, small deposits, CFAR within the window.
2. Advisory drift: a move to Level 3 would likely end the club's approval. Mitigation: force majeure clause naming Level 3; hold club deposit until flights confirmed.
3. Approval optics: "Egypt" plus "Red Sea dive-boat deaths" in the advisory. Mitigation: land-based, day-boat/shore only, chamber on site at Marsa Shagra, written safety plan.
4. Routing via Europe: winter disruption and long connections; Marsa Alam airport thin on European links. Mitigation: refundable fares, Hurghada fallback with 3 h transfer.
5. Beginners in January water (about 22–24 °C) and current at outer reefs. Mitigation: 5 mm suits included, house-reef training, AOW/Deep limits enforced.
